A · Lines

Every opening is one row of a table. Its moves sit in a column for each turn, so depth reads across the row and nothing folds.

The outline shows depth by indenting, and hides most of the tree behind arrows. Here the move columns do that job: a row that starts further right is further into the game, and the grey moves before it say how it was reached. Every opening is on screen at once, with names and progress each in a column of their own.

Unexplored lines stop being rows. Each opening carries a count of them, white and black, in the last column.

What works

  • Reads like a chess book's index of variations, which suits the newspaper look.
  • Names, progress and unexplored counts each line up, so the eye can run down one column.
  • It can be filtered and could be sorted. The outline can do neither.

What it costs

  • The grey lead-in moves repeat on every row. That is what makes filtering work, but it adds a lot of grey.
  • On a phone the columns go and each row becomes three short lines, so sixteen openings is a long scroll.
  • A line longer than about five moves for each side needs the table to scroll sideways, or narrower columns.

What it needs

  • A new component in public/src/openings.js in place of cb-outline. The server already sends everything: the columns come from splitting moves, the counts from grouping fog by parent.
